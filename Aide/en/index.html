BrunoGUI Dames help

BrunoGUI Dames is a front end for playing and analysing games of international draughts (10×10 board, squares numbered 1 to 50 in Manoury notation). You play against the Scan 3.1 engine by Fabien Letouzey or against a friend, and you load and save PDN games and FEN positions.

The program's screen is in French. In this help, buttons are named exactly as they appear on screen, followed by their English meaning the first time (see the table in section 2).

1. Quick start

  1. When the program starts, the board is ready: White is at the bottom and moves first. To start over at any time, click Nouvelle Partie (new game).
  2. Click the piece you want to move: it is « picked up » and the squares it can go to are marked. Click the destination square to play the move.
  3. With the Ordinateur (computer) box ticked (the default), the engine answers by itself after each of your moves. Untick it to play two humans on the same board.
  4. To save the game: Sauve Partie (save game, PDN format). To replay a game that has already been played: Charge Parties (load games).
The rules are those of international draughts: men capture forwards and backwards, kings are « flying », capturing is compulsory, and you must capture the greatest number of pieces (majority capture). The program enforces this: only the allowed pieces and squares can be played.

2. The main window

  • The board (left): the playable squares carry the numbers 1 to 50 of Manoury notation (from 1, at the top on Black's side, to 50, at the bottom on White's side).
  • The top banner: the names of the White and Black players (editable), and in the centre the status: « Trait aux Blancs » (White to move), « Trait aux Noirs » (Black to move), or the end of the game.
  • Under the banner: the score estimated by the engine and its line (the sequence of moves it expects), during or after its thinking.
  • Between that line and the board: the last move played (« Mouvement »), the possible captures (« Prises possibles »: the captures you must play, with the square of the captured piece and the landing square), the count of men and kings of each colour, the Résultat (result) group, the Ordinateur and Son (sound) boxes, and the thinking time setting.
  • The column of buttons (right, under the two clock times when the clock is active): see the following sections. While the engine is thinking, the buttons that would change the game are disabled.

3. Playing a game

Playing a move

A first click on one of your pieces selects it: its image becomes the mouse cursor, its square is coloured and the squares it can go to are marked. A second click on a destination square plays the move. The engine's move is animated: its piece glides from one square to the other; a multiple capture is animated step by step, and the captured pieces disappear together at the end. If you click a square the piece cannot go to, it returns to its place and nothing is played.

Markers on the board

Two markers are drawn on top of the square colours, so that you can see them without having to interpret the colours: a small dark brown triangle, in the bottom-left corner, marks the two squares of the last move played (yours, the engine's, or the move of the displayed position when you browse the game); and, when the selected piece has only one possible capture, its landing squares carry the order number of the steps (1, 2, 3…). With several possible captures the numbers would get mixed up: the « Choix de la rafle » window shows them one at a time.

Compulsory capture

When a capture is possible, only the pieces that can capture may be played, and only the captures that take the most pieces are allowed. The pieces that can capture are coloured as soon as it is your turn; click one of them to see where it can go. The captures are written in the « Prises possibles » area (in the form « 28 -> 19, 17 -> 10 »: the square of the captured piece, then the square where the piece lands).

Several captures possible for the same click

Two different captures can start from the same square and end on the same square while taking different pieces. A small « Choix de la rafle » (choice of capture) window then opens: the < Précédente (previous) and Suivante > (next) buttons show on the board the path of each capture and, in red, the pieces it takes; Jouer cette rafle (play this capture) plays it, Annuler (cancel) puts the piece back. In the PDN file, such a capture is written in full (« 44x22x13x24 ») so that it can be replayed without ambiguity.

A capture that returns to its own square

A piece can sometimes make a complete loop and come back to its starting square. To play such a capture, select the piece and then click its own square again. If no such capture exists, clicking its own square is not a move and the piece simply returns.

Promotion

A man becomes a king when it ends its move on the last row. If it only passes through it in the middle of a multiple capture, it stays a man and carries on.

Taking back a move

Retour Arrière takes back the last half-move. After taking back, the engine no longer answers your next move automatically: click Ordinateur joue to let it play again.

4. Playing the computer

  • Ordinateur (tick box): ticked, the engine plays its answer after each of your moves; unticked, you play two humans. Without an engine on the disk, this box is disabled and human-versus-human play is still possible.
  • Thinking time: from 1 to 25 seconds per move (5 by default). The longer it is, the stronger the engine plays.
  • Niveau (level; the list to the right of the time control, in the Pendule et temps de réflexion frame): the engine's strength, from Niveau 1 (very weak: it only looks one half-move ahead) to Maximum (the default: full strength). At the low levels the engine answers quickly, without using all its thinking time, and can be beaten; it keeps its opening answers, which are good. Position analysis always runs at full strength. The level is remembered in the preferences.
  • Ordinateur joue: the engine plays a move for the side to move, whichever it is. Handy to make it play White, or to be shown a move.
  • Analyse position: the engine thinks about the displayed position and gives its score (in centipawns) and best continuation, without playing anything. The best move is drawn on the board as a brick-red arrow (a broken line for a multiple capture), which follows the engine's thinking and stays displayed until the position changes.
  • Opening book: early in the game, Scan answers at once with a move from its opening book, without calculating, whatever the level; the program says so with « (livre) » at the end of the move-played line, or next to the move suggested by the analysis. Outside the book the engine calculates and the level matters. It can be used on any position, including while browsing a game.
  • Son (sound): ticked, a sound is played when the engine has moved.

The clock

The Pendule et temps de réflexion (clock and thinking time) frame contains, on the left, the list that chooses the clock's time control. By default it is Sans pendule (no clock): the game has no time limit and the two frames at the top right of the window show « --:-- ». With a time control they show the remaining time of B (White) and N (Black); the side to move is shown in green and turns red under 20 seconds. Time controls are written as in chess: « 80 min + 30 s » means 80 minutes plus 30 seconds gained at each move, « 90+30 min » means 90 minutes plus 30 minutes added at the 45th move. Those starting with FMJD are the official time controls of the World Draughts Federation (its annexes):

  • 1 h 20 + 30 s: the default of FMJD competitions, 1 h 20 per player plus 30 seconds gained at each move;
  • FMJD 90+30 min +30 s: 90 minutes for each player's first 45 moves, then 30 more minutes, and 30 seconds gained at each move from the 46th;
  • FMJD 120 + 60 min: the old standard, 2 hours for 50 moves, then 1 more hour for each series of 25 moves (unused time is kept);
  • Rapide 15 min + 5 s and Blitz 5 min + 3 s: the FMJD defines rapid as 15 to 30 minutes and blitz as 5 to 15 minutes per player; the 5 and 3 seconds gained per move are those of the world championships.

The clock starts at the game's first move, which is not counted: time then runs for the side to move, and only for it. When the flag falls, that player loses the game on time, and the result is written as for a win by captures. Against the engine, its thinking time counts on its clock: it never thinks longer than the thinking-time setting, and less when it has little time left. Changing the time control, Nouvelle Partie, loading a position or a game reset the clocks; Retour Arrière keeps the times (a player whose flag had fallen resumes with 30 seconds). Ticking a result yourself stops the clock. Hovering over the times and the list shows a tooltip.

The engine is Scan 3.1, by Fabien Letouzey. The Protocole button opens a window showing what the program and the engine send each other: useful to understand, or to report a problem.

5. End of game and draws

The program ends the game by itself in these cases, announcing the result and writing it in the Résultat group:

  • Win: a player has no pieces left, or still has pieces but cannot play any move (is blocked): that player loses.
  • Time: with a clock, the player whose flag falls loses the game.
  • Repetition: the same position occurs for the third time, with the same player to move. Draw.
  • King moves: during 25 moves by each player (50 half-moves in a row), only kings have moved, with no man moved and no capture. Draw.
  • Endings with few pieces: when a player has only a single king against, depending on the case,
    • three kings, or two kings and a man, or a king and two men: draw after 16 moves by each player;
    • two kings, or a king and a man, or a king: draw after 5 moves by each player.
    The count starts when the ending appears (FMJD rules 6.3 and 6.4).

In these cases, the message « Nulle possible, la partie est terminée » (draw possible, the game is over) gives the reason, and the result 1/2-1/2 is written.

A draw by agreement is not detected: if you agree with your opponent, tick 1/2-1/2 yourself in the Résultat group (likewise 1-0 or 0-1 for a resignation). The chosen result is added at the end of the move list.

6. Browsing a game

The Parcours partie group is used to review the current game or a loaded game, without changing it:

  • Préc. and Suiv.: one move back, one move forward; « Mouvement » shows the number and the displayed move; on the keyboard, the ← and → arrows do the same, Home and End go to the start and the end of the game (no effect while a name is being typed, during playback or while a piece is in hand);
  • Début: the starting position; Fin: the last position;
  • Lecture (play): plays the moves from the displayed position, with their animations. During playback the button becomes Pause: playback stops after the current move, and a new click on Lecture resumes where it stopped. During playback the other buttons are disabled and clicks on the board are ignored. At the end, a click on Lecture starts again from the beginning;
  • the slider under Lecture sets the speed: five levels, from 1.5 seconds to 0.08 seconds of pause between two moves (hover over it to see the level). It is remembered from one session to the next.

Analyse position works on the displayed position. To play, by clicking on the board or with Ordinateur joue, you must be on the game's last position: otherwise the program refuses and asks you to use Fin, because a move played elsewhere would not be the continuation of the game.

7. PDN files (games)

Loading games

Charge Parties reads a .pdn file and shows the list of its games (players, result, date, event…). Double-click a game: it is loaded at once and the board shows its starting position; Lecture plays it (see « Browsing a game »), Suiv. advances move by move. Masque parties hides or shows the list.

Reading is tolerant: tags on one or several lines, {…} comments, variations, move numbers stuck to the moves, various result markers. If a game contains an illegal move, it is loaded up to the last legal move, with a message. A file that is not PDN, or that contains a simple FEN position, is refused with an explanation.

Saving the game

Sauve Partie opens the save window: the game is written as PDN with its tags (players, result, number of moves, and the starting position if it is not the initial position). It can be appended to the end of an existing file.

The move list

Liste des coups shows the moves of the game in three notations: PDN (« 32-28 », « 28x19 »), FEN (the position after each move) and Hub2 (the engine's format). It lets you save the PDN.

8. FEN files (positions)

Sauve position writes the displayed position, and Charge position reads one and puts it on the board (the game restarts from that position). The format is the draughts one: W:W31-50:B1-20 means « White to move, white men on squares 31 to 50, black men on squares 1 to 20 »; a king is written with a K (for example W:WK47:BK4).

An invalid FEN file is refused, and your current game is not touched.

9. Turning the board

Tourner damier flips the board by 180°: by default you see the game from White's side; after the flip, from Black's side. The square numbers do not change, they flip with the pieces. The chosen orientation is remembered for next time.

Right-click on the board: « Enregistrer l'image du damier… » (save the board image) writes a 640 × 640 PNG file (pieces, colours and markers as on screen), « Copier l'image du damier » puts it in the clipboard (to paste it into a document), « Tourner le damier » does the same as the button. Shortcuts: Ctrl+N new game, Ctrl+Z take back, Ctrl+E save the image, Ctrl+F turn the board; no effect in a text box, during playback or when the matching button is inactive.

10. Preferences

When closing, the program remembers the thinking time, the players' names, the Ordinateur and Son boxes, the clock's time control and the board orientation. It restores them at the next start. They are written in the file BrunoGUI_Dames.preferences.ini, next to the program.

This file (text, one Key=Value line per setting) also lets you change, by hand, the duration of an animation step (DureeEtape, in milliseconds, 250 by default; 0 to play without animation) and the eight board colours (dark squares, light squares, departure, path, pivot, capture, arrival, step), written #RRGGBB. A missing or invalid value is ignored and replaced by the default. Close the program before editing the file.

Two display settings can also be changed in this file: RepereDernierCoup (last-move marker: coin by default, a dark brown triangle; cadre, a dark brown frame around the two squares; aucun, none) and ArriveesDebutTour (0 by default; 1 shows the arrival squares of all compulsory captures from the start of the turn, as in versions 1.10 and 1.11).

The players' names of a loaded PDN game do not become preferences.

The default highlights are greens and olives on the wooden squares, with the steps of a multiple capture in lavender. The square numbers are white with a black outline, so that they can be read on the squares as well as on the white and black pieces. Only the colours you have changed are written to the file.
